# AICP trademark and compatibility marks policy

> Maintainer review required: this is a conservative project usage policy for repository users and adopters. It does not create a certification program or expand any rights beyond applicable law.

## Project names and marks

"AICP" and related project names, logos, and badges are used in this repository to identify the project and its materials.

Unless the maintainers state otherwise, this policy should be read on the basis that project names and logos may be protected by trademark, unfair-competition, and similar laws even if a mark is not formally registered in every jurisdiction.

## Allowed nominative use

You may use project names to refer factually to the project, this repository, and implementations of the protocol, including in documentation, issue trackers, package metadata, talks, and comparative materials, so long as the use is truthful and does not imply sponsorship, endorsement, partnership, or certification.

Examples of generally allowed nominative use:

- "This gateway uses AICP transcript artifacts."
- "Our product exports AICP-compatible transcripts."
- "See the AICP repository for conformance suites."

## Compatibility and implementation claims

Truthful compatibility claims are allowed when they are grounded in actual support.

You may make statements such as:

- "compatible with AICP";
- "implements AICP profile X"; or
- "AICP-compatible"

only when the claim is backed by the relevant shipped repository artifacts, such as implemented protocol support, applicable profile support, and reproducible conformance evidence where the repository defines such evidence.

When making a compatibility claim, you should:

- identify the specific AICP version, extension set, binding, or profile where practical;
- avoid claiming support broader than what you actually implement; and
- avoid using badges, marks, or labels that suggest official certification unless the maintainers have expressly announced such a program.

Repository conformance/profile claims should be grounded in actual suite and profile support, not only in marketing copy.

## Prohibited uses

You may not:

- imply that the AICP project or maintainers endorse, sponsor, certify, or guarantee your product or service;
- use project names, logos, or badges in a way that is likely to confuse users about source, affiliation, or authorship;
- pass off your product, fork, or documentation as the official AICP project; or
- alter any project logo or badge in a way that creates a misleading impression of endorsement or official status.

## Logos, badges, and brand styling

Do not use project logos or badges more prominently than your own product or company name in a way that suggests the project is the source of your offering.

If you publish an "AICP-compatible" badge or similar label, it should be clearly descriptive, tied to actual repository-defined compatibility evidence where applicable, and should not resemble an official certification seal unless maintainers explicitly authorize one.

## Reservation of rights

This policy permits reasonable nominative and descriptive use. Except for those uses and any rights available under applicable law, the project maintainers reserve rights in project names, logos, and related brand assets.
